The public demo is the fastest way to see what reading in Aldus actually feels like — no server to install, no account to create.

## Get in

1. Open the demo site.
2. Select **Explore demo**. Aldus creates a fresh guest account behind the scenes — a random username, a private library membership, nothing you type in.
3. You land in the library. From here it behaves like any Aldus install: **Home** and **Discover** show the shelf, and opening a title offers **Read** or **Listen**.

If you'd rather sign in with an existing account on the same server instead of exploring as a guest, select **Sign in** from the same screen.

## What's in the library

The demo is stocked with complete public-domain classics — *Pride and Prejudice*, *Wuthering Heights*, *Jane Eyre*, *Moby-Dick*, and others — with real ebook and audiobook editions, not samples. At least one title has a full read↔listen alignment, so it's a good place to try [exact synchronization](/read-listen/synchronization/) firsthand: start reading, then switch to listening, and land on the same sentence.

## What expires

A guest visit is temporary by design:

- Your reading and listening progress, and anything you add to a collection, are private to that visit.
- The visit expires **24 hours** after it's created. Expired guest records and their personal activity are removed during automated cleanup, which may happen after access expires rather than at the exact 24-hour mark.
- There's no email or password attached to a guest account. The demo still processes a random guest identifier, activity, progress, and operational request logs while the visit exists.
- Operational request logs used to run, troubleshoot, and protect the demo follow the retention described in the [privacy policy](/privacy/).

Because guest account data is temporary, don't use the public demo as a place to keep anything you care about, and don't upload personal media to it — it's a shared, developer-operated instance for trying Aldus, not a free library host.

## Limits

Demo access is rate-limited so the shared instance stays usable for everyone trying it — if the demo is busy, Aldus will ask you to try again shortly rather than fail silently.
